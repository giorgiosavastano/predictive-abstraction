# The Art of Forgetting: From Borges to a Hypothesis About Intelligence

*6 November 2026. Code, raw results and the research note are in [the repository]({{ site.github.repository_url }}); everything below can be reproduced with three commands, and a one-minute demo is in the README.*

## 1. The man who remembered everything

In 1942, Borges wrote a story that stucked with me for more than 20 years. The sroty is about a young man in Uruguay, Ireneo Funes, who falls from a horse and wakes with a perfect memory. What is may sound at first as an incredibile super power, it is quickly revelaed to be instead an almost diabolic problem. Funes suprhuman lossless memory while allows him to rememer every single detail of everty second of his life, every leave he ever withness, it also effectivelly block him from being able to forget any useless detail, to the point that he is no able to generalized or create abstract concepts. Funes finds it hard to accept that the dog seen at 3:14 from the side is the same dog seen at 3:15 from the front.

From this first story I want you to take home the concept of the importance of forgetting, of compressing information down to usefull abstractions

## 2. Information is not understanding

The year before Borges wrote another story, called the Library of Babel. This story has been recently mentioned with respect to the wave of AI generated content flooding the internet. The story is about a library that contains all the possible book of 410 pages, created by forming all the possible combination of the letter of the alphabet. Technically, this imaginary library contains every book that is and will be ever written. Contains every usefull knoledge, from the cure of cancer to the origin of the universe. However it also contains a huge amount of non-sense, contraddictions and misleading facts. It contains the proofs of everythings and the opposite of everything. The fictional people living in this strange universe, quickly aboundon the original feeling of excitement that they could find any information they may need, and fall into a state of despair feeded by the fact that all this information is effectivelly unusable, and their search is pointless.

Here we are going to borrow the concept of distinction bettwen the existance of information, and its accessability.

## 3. Abstraction and compression in physics

There is a recent article showing the surprising similarity between the matematics of compression and the one behind the learning processes of the recent AI systems. The thought provocking and attention grabbing conclusion is that intelligence is compression. My idea is somewhat adjacent to this one, as finding abstractions can be seens as an information compression process: ignoring what is not useful and keep only what it is. We find examples of these usefull compressions everyweere in the low of physics that we use to describe reality and to solve specific problems. My favoirite example is in thermodinamic, where insted of following the evolution of billions of molecules of gas, we can simply keep track of 3 numbers, T, P, V, and these 3 numbers are enought to describe the gas in a termodinamic equilibrium.

So here we start seeing clearly an importan caveat, these abstractions may be task dependent, meaning they may be good to describe specific problems, and may not necessary be enought for describing every situation.

## 4. What if intelligence works similarly?

Put the three stories together and a working hypothesis suggests itself. Perhaps one important component of intelligence is recognizing the level of description at which a complex system becomes easiest to understand. A system that does this, when it meets a new situation, figures out quickly which details matter for what it is trying to do, builds a description that keeps those and drops the rest, notices that the description it built resembles one it has used before, and abandons it when it stops working. Not compression for its own sake, which is what Funes's opposite would be, a system that remembers nothing; but *abstraction*: compression that preserves the relationships that make the information useful. And, if the physics is any guide, the relationships worth preserving are often the ones the world has already organized itself around.
