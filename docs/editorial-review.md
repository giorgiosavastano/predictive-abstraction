## A. Writing style analysis

The practical style guide for this edit came from these features of the draft:

- Start from a personal connection. “A story that stucked with me for more than 20 years” explains why the subject matters to you before introducing a theory. In the continuation, I used the existing first-person account of building and questioning the experiment, without inventing personal experiences.
- Explain through something concrete before naming the general idea. The dog seen a minute apart, the library of books, and the gas described by T, P and V carry the explanation. Later sections follow the array, its faulty exchanges and the two prediction questions before interpreting the results.
- Bring the reader along explicitly. “From this first story I want you to take home…” and “So here we start seeing…” make the transitions conversational. I used direct transitions such as “For this I built a world…” and “Then I changed the question…”, rather than a formal survey of the literature.
- Keep your own position visible. “My idea is somewhat adjacent to this one” and “My favoirite example” place the reasoning with a person exploring a connection. The continuation uses “What I take from this…” and “I see this as evidence…” to distinguish interpretation from measurement.
- Let uncertainty do real work. “Perhaps one important component” and “these abstractions may be task dependent” leave room for the idea to fail. Later passages state what was supplied, what was measured, and what remains proposed.
- Allow a paragraph to develop an explanation. Your paragraphs often contain several connected clauses, followed by a shorter paragraph extracting the point. I retained that movement while giving the experimental comparisons their own paragraphs. I did not imitate spelling or grammatical slips as stylistic traits.

The tone is interested and explanatory, with ordinary vocabulary and occasional technical detail when an example needs it. Section 4 is denser than the preceding sections, so I did not treat its long sentence as a template for every later paragraph. I reduced dramatic labels, repeated claims and emphatic formatting in the continuation; the existing personal framing, uncertainty and scientific distinctions remain.

### 1. The library's completeness can include multiple volumes

- **Section and original quotation:** §2, “Technically, this imaginary library contains every book that is and will be ever written.”
- **Type:** Conceptual clarification about content versus a single physical volume.
- **Resolution:** Your subdivision argument is valid for any finite text expressible in the library's character set. Each segment of a longer text occurs among the possible 410-page volumes, with a shorter final segment padded if necessary. My initial objection was too strict once reconstruction across volumes is allowed. The text still does not become easy to locate or assemble, which supports your original distinction between availability and access.
- **Applied wording:** “In that sense, this imaginary library contains every book that has been or will ever be written in those characters: longer books could be divided across several volumes.” The preceding sentence identifies a fixed character set.
- **Source:** [Borges, *The Library of Babel*, story text hosted by the University of Central Arkansas](https://faculty.uca.edu/rnovy/Borges--Library%20of%20Babel.htm). The subdivision argument is mathematical reasoning from the exhaustive-string construction, not an additional claim attributed to Borges.
- **Severity/status:** Resolved clarification. The earlier classification as an essential correction to the completeness claim has been withdrawn for this multi-volume reading.

### 2. Proofs, absurd claims and incompatible arguments

- **Section and original quotation:** §2, “It contains the proofs of everythings and the opposite of everything.”
- **Type:** Conceptual clarification, with a spelling slip.
- **Why:** Contradictory arguments can both occur as text without both being valid proofs. The wording can acknowledge genuine proofs alongside nonsense without implying a valid proof of every contradictory conclusion.
- **Applied wording, following your proposal:** “It contains proofs, absurd claims and everything in between, including arguments for everything and the opposite of everything.”
- **Source:** [Borges's story](https://faculty.uca.edu/rnovy/Borges--Library%20of%20Babel.htm); the distinction between written arguments and validity is the editorial interpretation here.
- **Severity/status:** Recommended clarification; applied with your preferred phrasing.

### 4. State the assumptions behind the three gas variables

- **Section and quotation:** §3, “we can simply keep track of 3 numbers, T, P, V, and these 3 numbers are enought to describe the gas in a termodinamic equilibrium.”
- **Type:** Physical scope/important ambiguity.
- **Why:** This is a good macroscopic example for a specified simple gas with known composition and amount in a uniform equilibrium state. It is not a complete description of an arbitrary mixture, chemical reaction or microscopic configuration. For a fixed amount of an ideal gas, the equation of state also relates T, P and V, so the three are not independent. Giving three quantities is not itself an error.
- **Applied wording:** Kept the example of “3 numbers, T, P and V”, then specified a fixed amount of a simple gas of known composition and its macroscopic equilibrium state. Added the approved explanation that, for an ideal gas with fixed amount, the equation of state leaves only two of the three independent.
- **Source:** [MIT thermodynamics lecture clarification](https://web.mit.edu/16.unified/www/FALL/thermodynamics/mud/T1newmud.html), discussion of independent properties, equilibrium and amount of gas.
- **Severity/status:** Recommended clarification; applied without replacing your example.

### 5. Funes's dog example concerns naming and abstraction

- **Section and quotation:** §1, “Funes finds it hard to accept that the dog seen at 3:14 from the side is the same dog seen at 3:15 from the front.”
- **Type:** Literary interpretation/precision.
- **Why:** The story describes his discomfort with giving different perceptions the same name, alongside his difficulty with a general word covering different dogs. “Is the same dog” can instead suggest failure to recognize an individual's identity. Your use of the scene to explain abstraction is supported; this is a narrower ambiguity about its retelling.
- **Applied wording:** Replaced “is the same dog” with “has the same name as the dog”.
- **Source:** Borges, *Collected Fictions*, “Funes, His Memory”, as cited in the essay; compare [the Spanish excerpt published by UNAM's *Ciencias*](https://new.revistacienciasunam.com/component/content/article/funes-el-memorioso?catid=198). The online excerpt has a transcription discrepancy in the second time; it supports the naming distinction, not a change to your times.
- **Severity/status:** Recommended clarification; applied. “Almost diabolic problem” and the personal observation about twenty years remain as authorial choices.

### 6. Two sentences in section 1 have grammatical problems

- **Section and quotation:** §1, “What is may sound at first as an incredibile super power, it is quickly revelaed to be instead an almost diabolic problem.”
- **Type:** Grammar, with spelling slips.
- **Why:** “What is may” has an extra verb, and “it” duplicates the subject of the sentence. This is distinct from preferring a more elegant sentence.
- **Applied wording:** “What may sound at first like an incredible superpower is quickly revealed to be an almost diabolic problem.”
- **Severity/status:** Recommended correction; applied.

The next sentence had the same kind of problem:

- **Section and quotation:** §1, “Funes suprhuman lossless memory while allows him…”; “…it also effectivelly block him…”; “…he is no able to generalized…”.
- **Type:** Possessive form, clause construction and verb forms.
- **Why:** The concession beginning with “while” needs a subject, the main verb needs agreement, and “able to” takes the base form of the verb. Its length is not itself an error.
- **Applied wording, keeping the long explanatory structure:** “Funes's superhuman lossless memory, while it allows him to remember every single detail of every second of his life, every leaf he ever witnessed, also effectively blocks him from forgetting any useless detail, to the point that he is not able to generalize or create abstract concepts.”
- **Severity/status:** Recommended correction; applied without changing your interpretation of the story.
